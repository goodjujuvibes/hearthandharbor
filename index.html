WHY WE GATHER

The people are the point

Most guilds treat the people as the means and the loot as the end. We run it the other way around, the content is the pretext, and the company is the point. A dungeon is just the reason we all happened to be in one place long enough for something to happen between us. Monsters & Memories is built for exactly this: the slow travel, the forced grouping, the corpse runs, friction most games sand off. Here, that friction is the machinery that makes fellowship, and we take the game as it was designed and turn it into something worth belonging to.

And it's why we stay small. About 30–50 regulars, enough for groups, expeditions, and trades; few enough that the names on the roster stay familiar. We're not chasing the biggest guild on the server; one hundred is a ceiling, not a goal. A hearth is only warm for people you actually know, and staying small is what keeps it that way.

BEFORE YOU SET OUT

Questions from the road

Is Hearth & Harbor right for me?
+

You'll likely feel at home if you miss the worlds of EverQuest, Vanguard, and vanilla WoW; you'd rather learn a dungeon than race through it; you can laugh at a wipe and still help with the recovery; you want regular groups without a second job; and you value the people beside you as much as the loot ahead.

It's probably not for you if every activity is a race, you leave the group at the first setback, inexperience reads as weakness to you, loot matters more than relationships, or “casual” means no responsibility to the group. A small guild is defined as much by who doesn't join as by who does.

How big will the guild be?
+

Small by design: roughly 30–50 regulars, with room for friends and returning members whose activity comes and goes. Enough people for groups, expeditions, and trades; few enough that the names on the roster stay familiar. We're not chasing the biggest guild on the server; a hearth is only warm for people you actually know.

Do I have to keep a schedule or hit attendance?
+

No. There are no attendance requirements and no race to max level; real life comes first. We do hope the guild is more than a silent tag, though: when you're around, talk, group, explore, trade, ask questions, or invite someone along. Presence over attendance.

I'm new to the genre, or returning after years. Is that okay?
+

Very. Experienced players, newcomers, fast levelers, slow wanderers, crafters, and explorers are all welcome; character matters more than credentials. New members get walked through content, not handed a wiki link, and returning members find familiar names and roads still waiting. The main thing we ask is that you communicate and are willing to learn.

Is it hardcore or casual?
+

Neither, exactly. We want to explore dangerous places, defeat difficult encounters, earn meaningful gear, and eventually take on larger content; we're just not willing to sacrifice patience, people, or sanity to get there faster. Capable without contempt, relaxed but not careless: competence and good company in roughly equal measure.

MONSTERS & MEMORIES
THE FIRE WE RETURN TO
The Hearth
The hearth is return, rest, warmth, and fellowship, the fire that stays lit whether or not you've come in from the cold yet. It's the inn after the long road, the campfire between pulls, the familiar names in guild chat, and the people who come back when an expedition goes badly.
It carries the hearthstone tradition of classic World of Warcraft, and it's directly at home in Monsters & Memories, where campfires and inns grant real rest between expeditions: Warmth of the Campfire, Warmth of the Hearth.
And the hearth isn't only the inn back in town. Out on the road, a campfire kindled from gathered wood is a hearth of its own: somewhere to mend, share a meal, wait out the weather, and gather the group before the next pull. Wherever the company stops to rest, that is the hearth.
It agrees with the world itself. Aêthoril is a last bastion of warmth and light in a world that has seen too much catastrophe, and a hearth is the human-scale answer to that dark. You cannot bring the mother sun into the Deep. You can keep a fire.
The hearth is not the destination, though. It's what makes the frontier possible, a home for adventurers, not a retreat from adventure.
A place to return to.
THE PLACE WE SET OUT FROM
The Harbor
The harbor is shelter and departure both, the safe port at the edge of dangerous water where you provision up, meet your company, and launch the next expedition. It's the threshold between the known world and everything still waiting past the edge of the map.
It's the verb, too: to harbor good folks, to give them refuge. A harbor doesn't demand that every ship stay docked, its whole identity depends on people leaving and returning. Some sail out for weeks; the harbor is still there when they come back.
It agrees with the world's moment. Aêthoril is a continent ringed by black seas, its Age of Sundering ending and an Age of Discovery beginning, old routes reopening, ships venturing into dangerous water. A harbor is where the going-out begins.
The going-out and the coming-back are the same act, one idea pointed two ways. That is the whole guild in two words.

THE FULL CHARTER
The Hearth & Harbor Way
I. People over pace
We do not sacrifice people merely to move faster. A successful evening is not measured only by experience gained, sometimes it means learning a dungeon, recovering everyone's corpses, or simply a memorable night with good people.
II. Mirth and mettle
Old-school games require patience, competence, and resilience, and they produce absurd deaths, disastrous pulls, and stories that become funny only after everyone is safe. Bring enough mettle to face difficult content and enough mirth not to turn on one another when the group fails.
III. No one abandoned after the wipe
A run does not automatically end when something goes wrong. Whenever reasonably possible, we recover corpses, regroup, replace lost members, and get people safely home.
IV. Discovery without gatekeeping
Questions are welcome. Knowledge is shared, not hoarded. Some members discover everything organically; others appreciate maps and preparation. We respect both, and communicate before spoiling major discoveries.
V. Teach through companionship
A guide can answer a question; a guildmate walks the road with you. We favor shared experience over wiki links, learning camps, routes, and dungeons through actual play together.
VI. Capability without contempt
We want capable players who understand their classes, communicate clearly, and improve over time. We reject the idea that competence requires impatience, humiliation, or elitism. People are allowed to make mistakes, and expected to learn from them.
VII. Keep the harbor open
Real lives interrupt games. People level at different speeds, take breaks, and return after absences. One missed week or one slower character never places you outside the guild, returning members find familiar names and roads still waiting.
VIII. The company returns together
The group is not a temporary arrangement for extracting experience and loot. We begin together, communicate during the journey, and make sure everyone can return safely. The people beside us are part of the adventure.
