I. The fire and frontier

The hearth is the fire you come back to, for rest, safety, and good company. The warmth of home, the campfire in the wilderness, the lantern that lights the way into Eth-ur's Age of Discovery.

Together we explore that frontier, journeying across Aêthoril and down into the Deep to discover and brave whatever this sundered world has in store. We're a friendly, community-driven, family-style home base for people who want to go out into the world, have fun, and be in good company.

II. The road and return

The harbor stays open and the hearth stays lit for you. Real life comes first, so there are no attendance or playstyle requirements. People naturally play at different speeds, take breaks, chase and explore different things, and step away for a while. None of it puts you outside the company.

When you're around, take part: group up, talk, ask or answer questions, invite someone to explore, ask for help with a quest. A guild tag should be more than a silent name over your head.

III. The mirth and mettle

Bring enough mettle to face hard content and enough mirth to keep it fun when things go sideways. Nobody here is too good to laugh at a dumb death, including their own. We're here to enjoy the game and each other's company, so show up with a good attitude and everyone has a better time for it.

Be someone people want in the group: a real teammate who communicates, respects others, learns their class, adjusts with feedback, and acts with integrity, with guildmates and with anyone else you group with. Be the kind of teammate you'd want in your own group.

IV. The wipes and wisdom

Things will go wrong, both in real life and in the game, and that's just how it is. Life just happens with AFK emergencies, a group member who has to drop and needs replacing, or an evening going awry. And in the game? Bad pulls, wrong turns, wipes, and corpse runs are all Monsters & Memories working as designed. We recover, regroup, learn what we can, adjust, and move forward. A good attitude and a willingness to improve are what protect the integrity and camaraderie the guild runs on.

V. The Monsters & Memories

We're here to actually play Monsters & Memories: to take on content, delve into dungeons, journey on expeditions, and see the world of Eth-ur through Early Access and wherever it goes from there. To adventure, explore, earn gear, and go on quests. And to remember that the people beside you matter as much as the loot ahead. We'll fight the monsters and we'll make the memories. That's the name, and it's the whole idea: the guild is a good group to make them with, and a small part of the wider M&M community while we do.

All of it shared in good company at Hearth & Harbor.

BEFORE YOU SET OUT

Questions from the road

Is Hearth & Harbor right for me?
+

Probably, if you miss games like EverQuest, Vanguard, or vanilla WoW; you'd rather learn a dungeon than rush it; you can laugh at a wipe and still help with the recovery; and you value the people beside you as much as the loot ahead.

Probably not, if every night is a race, you leave the group at the first setback, or loot matters more than people. A small guild is shaped as much by who doesn't join as by who does.

How big will the guild be?
+

Small on purpose: roughly 30–50 regulars, plus friends and returning members who come and go. Enough for groups, expeditions, and trades; few enough that the names stay familiar.

Do I have to keep a schedule or hit attendance?
+

No. No attendance requirements and no race to max level, real life comes first. We just ask that you're more than a silent tag: when you're around, group up, talk, ask questions, or invite someone along.

I'm new to the genre, or returning after years. Is that okay?
+

Absolutely. Veterans, newcomers, fast levelers, slow wanderers, crafters, and explorers are all welcome; character matters more than credentials. New members get walked through content, not handed a wiki link, and returning players find familiar names still around. We just ask that you communicate and stay willing to learn.

Is it hardcore or casual?
+

Neither, exactly. We want to take on dangerous places, tough encounters, and real gear, we're just not willing to trade patience, people, or sanity to get there faster. Competence and good company in equal measure.

MONSTERS & MEMORIES
THE FIRE WE RETURN TO
The Hearth
The hearth is return, rest, warmth, and fellowship, the fire that stays lit whether or not you've come in from the cold yet. It's the inn after the long road, the campfire between pulls, the familiar names in guild chat, and the people who come back when an expedition goes badly.
It's directly at home in Monsters & Memories, where campfires and inns grant real rest between expeditions: Warmth of the Campfire, Warmth of the Hearth.
And the hearth isn't only the inn back in town. Out on the road, a campfire kindled from gathered wood is a hearth of its own: somewhere to mend, share a meal, wait out the weather, and gather the group before the next pull. Wherever the company stops to rest, that is the hearth.
Aêthoril is a last bastion of warmth and light in a world that has seen too much catastrophe, and a hearth is the human-scale answer to that dark. You cannot bring the mother sun into the Deep. You can keep a fire.
The hearth is not the destination, though. It's what makes the frontier possible, a home for adventurers, not a retreat from adventure.
A place to return to.
THE PLACE WE SET OUT FROM
The Harbor
The harbor is shelter and departure both, the safe port at the edge of dangerous water where you provision up, meet your company, and launch the next expedition. It's the threshold between the known world and everything still waiting past the edge of the map.
It's the verb, too: to harbor good folks, to give them refuge. A harbor doesn't demand that every ship stay docked, its whole identity depends on people leaving and returning. Some sail out for weeks; the harbor is still there when they come back.
Aêthoril is a continent ringed by black seas, its Age of Sundering ending and an Age of Discovery beginning, old routes reopening, ships venturing into dangerous water. A harbor is where the going-out begins.
The going-out and the coming-back are the same act, one idea pointed two ways. That is the whole guild in two words.
